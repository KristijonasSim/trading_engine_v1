- Author: C. Kenneth Jones
- Summary: <p><span>Time horizon mean-reversion dimensions are added to asset pricing theory. Mean-reversion risk arbitrage pricing theory (A-APT) is static and describes single-period risk in the frequency domain with long-horizon contributions. Mean-reversion risks are variances of returns for multiple horizons. The Fourier transform converts finite-length returns into a finite number of mean-reversion risks. Mean-reversion risks are harmonic, uncorrelated, non-overlapping, and form an exact K-factor structure APT. Mean-reversion risk arbitrage portfolios are sensitive to one mean-reversion length risk. The mean-reversion risk capital asset pricing model (A-CAPM) prices mean-reversion risks using systematic risks for multiple horizons. Mean-reversion risk premiums and betas define expected return.</span></p>
